# evryCore (`evry`)

What we boot. This branch is `game` (retail parity, no modules) plus drop-in `modules/` and playerbots.

`master` in this repo fast-forwards from [TrinityCore](https://github.com/TrinityCore/TrinityCore) `master`. Permanent merge is one-way: `game` → `evry`. Never the other way. After work lands on `game`, merge `game` into `evry` to boot it.

## What this branch is

TrinityCore is an MMORPG framework in C++, derived from MaNGOS, with a long history of optimizing the codebase and in-game mechanics. evryCore stays on that `master` line (current retail client). `game` holds retail-parity core. `evry` is that tree plus modules.

Community involvement on upstream TrinityCore is unchanged: ideas and code for TrinityCore itself go to [TrinityCore.org](https://www.trinitycore.org) and [TrinityCore pull requests](https://github.com/TrinityCore/TrinityCore/pulls).

This repository: [github.com/evrydayjunglist/evryCore](https://github.com/evrydayjunglist/evryCore).

The selected playerbots product and technical direction is in
[Playerbots architecture](PLAYERBOTS_ARCHITECTURE.md). The direct-switch and RTS
implementation tracker is in [Commander Mode](COMMANDER_MODE.md).

## Jobs

When a job lands, add a short entry on the parent that owns it, with a completion percent. 100% means the listed feature is in and only bug fixes remain. Copy new `game` entries here after `game` is merged into `evry`.

### From `game`

- **pandaren-faction-choice** (~80%) — A neutral pandaren with "A New Fate" in progress can choose the Horde or the Alliance. The server takes the client's choice, changes the race and faction, teaches the new race's languages and capital flight paths, moves reputation onto the new race's base, answers the client, and casts the retail faction-choice spell that gives the quest credit. While "A New Fate" is in her quest log, the Spirit of Master Shang Xi offers "I'm ready to decide.", which opens the choice window, and he offers "A New Fate" again to a player who closed it. Still open: the balloon ride and the teleport to Orgrimmar or Stormwind that follow the choice are not scripted.
- **retail-spell-resistance** (~100%) — Retired resistance stats no longer remove retail spell damage. Armor, explicit school immunities, absorbs and scripted damage reductions remain separate. Regression tests cover legacy creature resistance values, explicit damage accounting and immunity.
- **combat-stats** (~75%) — Player base stamina, health, and STR/AGI/INT from ExpectedStat.db2, including the Midnight health and primary-stat squish at current-expansion levels. `GiveLevel` uses the same overrides as login. Creature MaxHealth uses floor so create health matches retail. `DealDamageMods` scales player damage into create-level creature HP and creature damage onto the level-matched DPS curve. `ScalingPlayerLevelDelta` is refreshed after NextLevelXP is known. Hunter, Shaman, Warlock, Monk, Druid, Death Knight, and Evoker still use an average secondary split. The Midnight squish is a level-80 sniff constant.
- **chromie-time** (~90%) — Chromie Time select, persist, gossip, and leave. Campaign breadcrumbs after select in `chromie_time_expansion_quest`. Outdoor scaling uses ConditionalContentTuning. Dungeon Finder hides and locks dungeons whose expansion bit is outside `ChromieTimeExpansionMask`. Select requires the Chromie Time interaction. Set and clear refresh phase so terrain swaps update. Silithus Wound shows unless the player is in a pre-BfA campaign. Chromie says the BroadcastText line on select; Shadowlands has no id yet. Start and re-enter from the present lock at level 68; already in a campaign may change timelines until the ContentTuning end level. Dorn refuse vs FAQ. Orgrimmar hourglass. Dark Portal Eastern Kingdoms ↔ Outland. Character wipe deletes `character_chromie_time` so a reused GUID does not inherit the old expansion. Player dumps still omit that table. Other terrain swaps, raid walk-in abort, and the level-80 return quest are still open.
- **treasure-picker** (~80%) — `CMSG_QUERY_TREASURE_PICKER` replies from world `treasure_picker` rows. Offer and grant filter by AllowableClass, weapon/armor skill when AllowableClass is -1, AllowableRace, and faction item flags. Turn-in grants the first eligible row, or the chosen item on a choice picker (`CMSG_QUEST_GIVER_CHOOSE_REWARD` accepts that picker item). Arathi RPE 90882–90887 and Demon Hunter Mardum unique pickers (40077, 38759, 38765, 40222, 38728) have contents. A quest that lists a picker id with no row still gets an empty reply so the quest frame opens. Most advertised pickers still have no world row. Gold, currency, and War Mode bonus fields are still unused.
- **warband-camps** (~90%) — Char-select camps persist on the Battle.net account. Enum sends them. A first visit creates Favorites with the default scene and character slots from WarbandScenePlacement. `CMSG_SETUP_WARBAND_GROUPS` at char select writes create, rename, reorder, scene, and members. Server-owned group ids. Unowned scenes are rejected. Deleted characters leave the camp. Camp setup matches living char-select guids.
- **warband-currency** (~85%) — Account-wide currencies persist on the Battle.net account and overlay onto every character of that account. Login merge keeps the larger of a leftover character row and the account pool, and does not call GetMap before the player has a map. Alt transfer UI, handlers, and log are gated by `FeatureSystem.AccountCurrencyTransfer.Enabled`. Transfer packet layout is still unverified on 12.1. Player dumps do not export the Battle.net account pool, so AccountWide currency does not round-trip through a character dump.
- **arathi-rpe** (~50%) — Catch Up on map 2927. Character-select RPE after a 60-day inactivity stub relocates to the Hammerfall pad. Adventure Guide `CMSG_ENCOUNTER_JOURNAL_START_ARATHI_RPE` casts 1260320 with no inactivity gate (level 20). Pad Thrall/Jaina, keep gnolls, farm openers 90882–90887, pad scenes 3692/3749, pumpkin and catapult spellclicks, and mount credit 239009 on that map. Later Catch Up after 90887, Leave Catch Up, hiding the old quest log, Stuck Ogre 253460, the Stromgarde siege, and the finale choice are still open.
- **archaeology** (~80%) — Survey, private finds, fragment loot, and client project-solve casts. Research* and QuestPOIPoint DB2 load with unsigned point parent IDs, sites persist with hidden find coordinates, and criteria types 3/4/6 plus modifiers 65/66 score. Site-to-branch is server policy (ResearchSite.db2 has no join). Four sites per seeded map (Eastern Kingdoms, Kalimdor, Outland, Northrend, Pandaria) when Archaeology is trained. Fragment crates, Chromie Time coupling, later continents, historical skill gates, and a sniffed find/keystone rate are still open.
- **area-spirit-heal** (~90%) — Waiting to Resurrect and Spirit Heal mana are allowed on every map. Spirit Heal 22012 resurrects ghosts who queued at that area spirit healer. Mardum (map 1481 / GhostZone 7705) has graveyard links and Spirit Healer 65183 at the named graveyards. Vault of the Wardens intro (1468) is not in this job.
- **avatar-overfiend** (~50%) — A spell proc that fires on a successful cast now honours its `spell_proc` family filter, which the core skipped for those events, so such a proc no longer fires on every spell. Avatar of Destruction summons its Overfiend when Soul Fire is cast instead of on every cast. Its other trigger, a chance to summon one when opening a Dimensional Rift, is still open.
- **class-abilities** (~70%) — Imported DK, Evoker and Demon Hunter ability work, shared summon/support/leech/school modifiers, Fel Rush movement, Priest/Rogue repairs and class-proc crash guards. Review fixes cover ally buff ownership, delayed damage, independent Immolation Auras, duplicate procs and zero-Fury form expiry. Current gameplay acceptance, some empowered Evoker healing, inferred talent tuning and other unfinished source-track abilities remain open. [Scope, evidence and owner checks](CLASS_ABILITIES.md).
- **battlepay** (~85%) — Classic Store glue and the free level-80 boost. Character-select packets use the 12.1.0 client layout with size checks, so the boost catalog no longer crashes the client. Fully deleting a character gives back a boost that was assigned to it but not yet applied, and unlinks a used boost from its GUID, so a new character that reuses the GUID is not boosted; login also releases boosts left on characters deleted before this fix. A boost is applied when it is assigned at character select, while the character is offline: ownership, specialization, the boost itself and room for the whole kit are checked first, then the level, specialization, destination, gold, new gear and used boost are saved together. Worn gear, bags and their contents are mailed and the backpack is kept; if the kit does not fit, nothing is used up. A boost assigned before this change is applied the same way when the character list loads, or given back to the account if it cannot be.
- **timerunning** (~35%) — Configurable seasons in worldserver.conf with reload, a persistent season per character, separate seasonal world copies in the same realm, and the party, trade, mail, bank and map restrictions that keep seasonal and ordinary play apart. Pandaria Timerunners start at level 10 on the Timeless Isle in the reconstructed Remix opening: quest NPCs on Blizzard's quest markers, the opening quests and rift object, Blizzard's conversation text, and a startup check that keeps creation closed until that content is complete. Creating a Mists of Pandaria Remix character currently requires a custom memory injection into the client: build 12.1.0.69497 rejects season 1 in two class checks, and a launch helper kept outside this repository (`Play evry.cmd`) changes one byte in each check in the running game's memory, never the file on disk. Time Rifts are invisible and Infinite Ravagers stand still. Most thread spells, the cloak's stat conversion, Archaios' summoning, Eratus' abilities and the retail hand-off to Pandaria are still open, as are full Remix progression, dungeons, raids, rewards and conversion. Legion is scheduling and client presentation only.
- **looted-consumables** (~50%) — Consumables whose effect fires when looted are applied on loot instead of stored when their only other effects are use effects without charges: Remix threads and mementos, Dragon Isles supply herbs, companion experience, Kaja'Cola drinks, mysterious potions, delve boons and blessings. Retail prints a loot line for at least the Dragon Isles herbs; this does not yet. Dragon Isles herbs grant no supplies without spell loot rows, companion experience uses the spell's raw amounts, and boons, most drinks and the blessings need spell scripts.
- **housing** (~65%) — Player housing for the Warband, built from agatho's housing branch, the owner's own 12.0.7 retail captures and the older local housing track. A house belongs to the Battle.net account: one per district (Founder's Point and Razorwind Shores), every character of the account enters and decorates it as owner, and the character who bought it is shown as its owner. Start Tutorial, the free first house at a plot's cornerstone, entering and leaving through the door, Teleport Home, the house exterior built piece by piece as retail sends it, exterior fixture editing and moving the house, Warband decor storage with placing, removing and redeeming owed decor, relinquishing (the house is packed and comes back on the next purchase), eviction and owner deletion (packed, refund paid), charters and guild neighborhoods, and the house level and budget table. Housing requests are only accepted from where a player could make them. Razorwind Shores' town is spawned from the owner's captures; Founder's Point uses 12.0.1 captures by others. Endeavors cannot advance yet (five criteria modifiers are unidentified), houses stay at level 1 until the General Contractor is captured, only house styles unlocked by default can be used, and the Alliance "My First Home" and spawn phases are still open.

### On `evry` only

- **catalogshop** (~60%) — `mod-catalogshop` serves the in-game Shop locally from captured retail catalog responses. The stock client browses it over HTTPS on localhost (IPv4 and IPv6); it stays off until the module is enabled, so the classic Store runs without it. Free Buy completes a checkout for the level-80 boost and passes it to BattlePay through a signal file; other products are refused. Buy needs `us.checkout.battle.net` pinned to 127.0.0.1, port 443 needs an elevated worldserver, and the localhost certificate is made and trusted by hand. On `evry` a level-80 boost is applied to the offline character before success is reported: ownership, offline state, specialization, entitlement and bag space are checked first, then the level, specialization, destination, gold, new gear and used entitlement are saved together and replaced gear is mailed. Timerunners are refused.
- **modules** (~95%) — Drop-in folders at `modules/<name>/` (code, `conf/`, `data/sql/`). CMake `MODULES=static` (default) or `none`. Module `.conf.dist` copies to a runtime `modules/` directory next to the server. `mod-example` is the template.
- **playerbots** (~75%) — Bots are real characters, logged in through an in-process session with no network connection, and they only act by queueing the packets a real client would send. Today they quest on their own: talk to quest givers they can see within 40 yards, fight what attacks them (something hitting her wins over a quest mob she is only walking to, and she does not swap every tick to a closer attacker), loot corpses, use quest objects and quest items, and walk to the nearest objective marker, finished-quest `?`, or takeable `!` in their zone. They judge gear by stat weights for their specialization (the module brings a table for every specialization, and `Playerbots.Gear.Weights.<id>` in the conf changes any number in it), wear their class armor first, and only consider gear the server says they can use with the weapon and armor skills they have learned. When an item reaches their bags, and when they gain a level, they put on the best upgrade in their bags out of a fight, one piece at a time with the same equip click a player makes, and only for a piece that scores at least `Playerbots.Gear.UpgradeMarginPct` better (1% by default), so they never swap back; a two-hand weapon is weighed against both hands. When a quest offers a choice of rewards they take the biggest upgrade by the same rule; with no upgrade on offer they take one they can use, else the one worth the most at a vendor, and they never sell a vendor a piece they would put on. In a fight they auto-attack and press the damaging spells they know, one press per global cooldown. Below half health in a fight they first cast a heal, shield or damage cut they know on themselves, keeping spells with a cooldown of a minute or more for below 30%. Below 30% health with two or more monsters hitting her and no heal or shield left to press, she runs away from them in 30-yard stretches, steering clear of other hostile monsters she can see, until nothing is hitting her; if they still are after 150 yards she turns and fights. They walk the navmesh but step on the real ground: no climbing steeper than 35°, no long drops, no walking through walls or objects, and they walk around a small lip or make one checked normal jump over it. A spot only counts as ground if her whole body fits there, so she walks round a low wreck instead of under it, and a bot that starts a walk already in such a pocket stands once onto the surface over her and walks off. When a route is refused and the small look around her feet cannot get her past it, she stops, maps the ground she can walk within 20 yards by those same rules (60 yards when that ground carries on past 20 yards and holds no way round), walks the best way round it finds toward her target (judging each step the way her heartbeats walk it), and asks the navmesh again from there. If nothing she can reach is closer, which is what being in a pocket looks like, she asks the navmesh for a route from the ways out of that ground and walks to the first one whose route she can actually start walking, even though it leads away from her target. For a bot that is stuck, a GM can type `.playerbots walkmap [yards] [name]` to get a picture page, written next to the server logs, of all the ground she can walk to from where she stands, which of it she cannot walk back from, and where and why each step out of it is refused. When the server roots, stuns, fears, knocks back, or teleports them, they answer the way a real client does: they stay put while held, fly a knockback's arc and land where it meets the floor (taking any fall damage), and send no movement until a teleport has finished. If their navmesh path keeps ending short of a target, they give that target up and look for other work instead of walking back and forth. A target they give up on is skipped only for a while: after two minutes with nothing to do they forget what they skipped and look again, and when the trouble is the spot they stand on (no step anywhere from it), the target is not skipped at all; they wait five seconds and look again. A bot also remembers, for ten minutes, each place where a walk of hers failed after every way round had been tried, and which way she was going there; a new route past that place the same way is given up before she walks it instead of climbing back up the same hillside every minute; only that spawn or marker point is skipped, never the whole quest objective, and her next look tries the next spot of the same work. These are her own memories, not shared with other bots, and reaching a map marker does not wipe them. A bot that cannot step anywhere from where she stands writes once, for that spot, what refuses each of the eight steps round her. If one of those steps is a drop, she walks off the edge the way a player would, falls with ordinary falling movement and lands with the same packet a client sends, so the server applies any fall damage; she only does it when the fall leaves her above half health (or ends in deep water) and lands her where her body fits and she can step on. A quest giver hundreds of yards away is no longer dropped because the quick route check before a walk ran out of room; the walk's own long search decides. While a bot has nothing to do, the log says for each finished quest why its turn-in was not picked. On death they release, run back and take their corpse, and use the spirit healer only when a player would. After a fight, below 60% health or 40% mana with nothing fighting her, she sits until both are full before she walks or pulls again, eating food and drinking water from her bags when she has some (she does not buy it). When bags are nearly full of junk or gear is nearly broken they sell and repair at a vendor with their own gold. When a quest's rewards will not fit in her bags, she goes to a vendor first, sells her junk and, if that is not enough, the items she can spare that are worth least, and then walks back to turn the quest in. A player can invite a bot to a party: she answers the invite window after a moment with the same accept or decline a client sends, and `Playerbots.InvitePolicy` decides whom she accepts (`Nobody`; `GameMaster`, the default; `Peer`, game masters and players within five levels of her, or within 15 average item levels once both are at the top level; or `Anyone`). Once in a party led by a human player on her map, she stops her own quest work and follows the leader, walking after them when they are more than 10 yards away and stopping about 3 yards short; she fights what the leader is attacking or what is hitting anyone in the party, still fights back first when something hits her, loots corpses she may loot, and rests beside the leader between fights. She does not walk after a dead leader or one on a flight path, and when the leader leaves her map or logs out she goes back to questing. `playerbots.exe` builds with the module and, with `Playerbots.LoginMode = Coordinator`, decides when bots are online; it does not make any gameplay decisions yet. A bot kicked by a GM or by AntiDOS logs back in after 30 seconds (in Coordinator mode, while `playerbots.exe` owns the roster); like a player, it stays offline while its account or character is banned, the world is closed, or the realm only admits higher security levels. The server can load a second set of movement maps built for what a player's body can walk instead of what a creature's can, from a `playermmaps` folder beside the usual one, and bots ask their routes of it on every map it covers; where it has not been generated they fall back to the creature set and nothing changes. The map generator takes `--maxClimb` for building that set; Kalimdor's has been generated. Each route a bot starts walking, and each route her recovery tests, is logged with a sentence saying what the navmesh search did: whether it reached the destination, ran out of room to search, found only the ground joined to where she stands, or found a route longer than a path can hold, and how long it took to build. Bots search their walks with room for a long route, in search objects of their own: up to 16,384 search steps and paths of up to 1,024 pieces, about 4,000 yards of road, where creatures keep the 1,024 steps and 74 pieces they have always had. Settings are in `modules/mod-playerbots.conf` next to the server (`Playerbots.Enable`, `Count`, `Races`, `Classes`, the creation switches and `HordePercent` (see playerbot-creation-switches), `InvitePolicy`, `Gear.UpgradeMarginPct`, `Gear.Weights.<id>`, `LogDetail`, `DeleteBots`). With `Playerbots.DeleteBots = 1` the next start deletes every bot character, bot game account and bot Battle.net account (only emails of exactly `PLAYERBOT<number>@PLAYERBOTS.LOCAL`), waits until the database shows it done, and stops; set it back to 0 and the next start makes new bots from the settings at that time. Both the wipe and the making of bots at startup work a little each world tick, so the server keeps running instead of freezing; 1,000 new bots took about six and a half minutes. Every minute `Playerbots.log` reports what the bot brains cost the world tick (average, slowest ticks, and the slowest single bot), and `.playerbots stats` shows the last report in game. The same report says which steps the bot updates spent that minute on, and a single bot update over 50 ms, or a whole playerbot update over 250 ms, writes one line naming the steps that took the time. A bot with nothing to do, or walking, looks around for new work once a second instead of on every world tick, spread over the bots; being hit, death and server movement orders still get an answer on the tick they happen. Only bots named in `Playerbots.LogDetail` write every step to the log; the rest write only what they decide and what goes wrong. Bots that stop to map the ground for a way round take turns in the order they stopped, two at a time, and waiting for a turn does not count against the 20 seconds a map may take; the minute report also says how many looked, why, and how those looks ended. Before a bot on quest work joins that line, she looks once for another spawn or spot of the same objective and walks there instead if there is one. Picking the next map work looks only at the loaded creatures of the entries each quest needs (a spawn list by map and entry, built once from the world database), not at every creature loaded on the continent, and at most eight bots pick in one world tick, and none start once that tick's picks have taken 10 ms; the rest wait in line in the order they asked and pick on a later tick, and the minute report says how many picked and how long the longest wait was. Picking map work never loads a map grid: work in a part of the map nobody is near is found by its quest marker, and she walks to that marker, including a finished quest's `?`, and the ground and its spawns load as she comes near, as they do for a player. A floor look asks the core for the floor once instead of twice, which makes every walk heartbeat and the way-round map cheaper, and the minute report says how long a finished way-round map took. A new bot takes a name from the game's list for her race, and when those are all taken, a made-up name built from syllables, so any number of bots of one race can be created. A bot goes to sell her junk once her bags have four free slots or fewer, and a map marker she stood on with nothing there waits until what should be there has had time to respawn before she walks to it again. A bot takes a step down steeper than she could climb back only when the next yards of her route are walkable, or it ends where she can still step, so she no longer walks down into cracks and hollows she cannot climb out of. A bot that has kept failing to step anywhere near one place for two minutes uses her Hearthstone, as a stuck player does, and the server casts it and moves her home. Picking a place to stand beside a target asks the navmesh about the nearest sides first and stops once no other side could have a shorter route, and the same pick from the same feet about the same target within three seconds is answered from memory. Looking for a takeable `!` checks the nearest quest givers first and stops at the first one she can take a quest from, and looking for a quest item on a gameobject looks only at loaded objects of the entries that hold that item, not at every object loaded on the map. With `Playerbots.MapThreadBrains = 1` (off by default) each map's update thread runs the brains of the bots on that map, and the world thread keeps logins, `playerbots.exe`, commanded bots, bots between maps and party invites; at 1,000 bots with four map threads this took the brains off the world thread and caused no errors in 15 minutes, but the world tick fell only about 7 ms, because most starting zones are on Kalimdor and one map's bots still run one after another on its thread. Bots no longer pile onto one spot beside a quest giver, vendor, monster or spirit healer: each bot has her own side of each target, up to 60° round from the way she comes, and her own distance, 1 to 3 yards past its reach (still inside melee and talk range), and she does not take a spot where she can see another player standing. Her waits differ from other bots' too: the pause after a quest click or at a vendor (0.5 to 2 seconds), releasing her spirit (2 to 6 seconds), answering a party invite (1 to 3 seconds), standing up after a rest (up to 3 seconds), and the time between looks for new work (0.8 to 1.4 seconds), so bots that arrive together do not all act on the same tick. A walking bot still checks the ground every 100 ms but sends only her newest movement heartbeat each world tick, as a client that hitched does; after a slow tick she used to send a burst of heartbeats that all carried the same time, and players saw every bot snap back and forward together. Still open: healing others, buffs, potions, pets, class rotations, and backing out of minimum range; running thousands of bots (at 1,000 bots in the starting zones the world tick was about 86 ms, and bots need to be spread over more maps before the map threads help much); and the external brain for long-term activity, groups, guilds, professions, and economy (see [Playerbots architecture](PLAYERBOTS_ARCHITECTURE.md)).
- **playerbot-creation-switches** (~50%) — Which new bots may be made is now set by on/off switches in `modules/mod-playerbots.conf`: `Playerbots.Horde` (on by default), `Alliance`, `AlliedRaces`, `Dracthyr`, `DeathKnight`, `DemonHunter`, `Evoker`, `HeroClass`, `CoAClasses`, `Pandaren` and `Worgen` (all off by default), plus `Playerbots.HordePercent` (50), the share of the roster that should be Horde when both factions can be made; each new bot goes to the faction furthest below its share, counting the bots that already exist. A bot is made only when every switch she needs is on, `Playerbots.Races` and `Playerbots.Classes` allow her, and she passes the same create checks a player gets, including the Demon Hunter and Evoker level and Evoker count rules. Only the Horde has passed its playtest, so with the defaults the bots are the same as before. `Playerbots.Alliance = 1` makes Human, Dwarf, Night Elf, Gnome and Draenei bots, and Worgen too with `Playerbots.Worgen = 1`; the bot brain cannot yet do the Dwarf gyrocopter ride, the Gnome start's spellclick, gossip and teleport, or Gilneas's vehicle and pet-bar steps, so those bots will probably stop partway (`Playerbots.log` says so once when Alliance is on, and once more for Worgen when both are on). `Playerbots.AlliedRaces = 1` makes the allied races, Earthen and Haranir of each faction that is on; they start at `StartAlliedRacePlayerLevel` (10) in their own home, as a player does, and the bot brain cannot yet do the Earthen start's extra action button and gossip or the Haranir start's gossip and spellclick, so those bots will probably stop partway (`Playerbots.log` says so once when the switch is on). `Playerbots.DeathKnight`, `DemonHunter`, `Evoker` and `Dracthyr` make those classes and that race (a Dracthyr Evoker needs both Dracthyr and Evoker). Most Death Knights start in Acherus, whose quests ride vehicles, and Pandaren and allied-race ones on a start that needs an extra action button and gossip; Demon Hunters start in Mardum and Evokers in the Forbidden Reach, whose starts move on only when the client says the intro scene finished, which the bot brain does not say yet. So those bots will probably stop partway, and `Playerbots.log` says so once for each switch that is on. A Death Knight bot now waits for her runes before she presses a spell that costs them, as the server would refuse it otherwise, and an Evoker presses an empowered spell once and the server lets it go at its last stage. `Playerbots.HeroClass` makes evryCore's Hero class and `Playerbots.CoAClasses` the Conquest of Azeroth classes (Reaper today, for Human, Undead, Troll, Blood Elf and Draenei), each in her race's own start, before either class has passed its own gameplay acceptance: a new Hero has no class spells and no starting kit, so she may have no weapon and no Hearthstone, and the bot cannot choose a Hero mode; a Reaper presses Reap and Murder like any other damage spell; and a player without the evry client changes sees these bots wrongly. `Playerbots.log` says so once for each switch that is on. A name in the race or class list that a switch excludes is logged with that switch, and switches and lists that leave nothing to make give one error naming every key. With both factions off no new bot is made and existing bots still log in. Switches never change, log out or delete a bot that already exists. A Pandaren bot is never made as a Horde or Alliance Pandaren: she is to start as a neutral Pandaren on the Wandering Isle and, when the server opens the faction choice at its end, click the faction the roster split and the switches give her. Pandaren bots are made only with `Playerbots.Pandaren = 1` (off by default). The bot brain cannot yet do most of the Wandering Isle's quests (balance poles, spirit and cart clicks, a balloon ride, escorts), so a Pandaren bot will probably stop partway; `Playerbots.log` says so once when the switch is on. The Horde Pandaren bots made before this change stay as they are. The Alliance starts were playtested: Night Elf and Draenei bots quest on, Dwarf and Gnome bots stop in their start valleys, and the Human start has not been rolled yet. The allied-race starts were playtested too: every bot is made at level 10 in her own home; Haranir bots quest on; Highmountain, Zandalari, Nightborne and Earthen bots find no first quest to take; Kul Tiran bots stop on quest 47181, whose objective credit creature has no spawn; the Lightforged bot stops at a turn-in in Stormwind on another map, which a bot cannot travel to yet. The Death Knight, Demon Hunter, Evoker and Dracthyr starts were playtested for ten minutes: Death Knight bots in Acherus take their first quest and stop on the steep ground on the way to the next quest giver, Demon Hunter bots stand where they start in Mardum, and the one Dracthyr Evoker got out of the intro and stopped at a ledge on the way to her first quest giver. The Hero and Reaper starts were playtested for thirty minutes: the Undead and Blood Elf Hero bots and the Undead Reaper quested on and reached level 3 or 4 (one Hero walked on to Brill), the Orc and Goblin Hero bots handed in two quests each and then stayed stuck on steep ground, the Hero bots had nothing equipped and no Hearthstone, and the two Troll Reapers never got a first quest, because every first quest at the Troll start is for one ordinary class only.
- **playerbots-fight-back-players** (~90%) — A bot fights back against a player who attacks her, and only then: a player swinging at her, or one in combat with her who has her selected (a caster or hunter never swings), who is alive, on her map, visible to her and a valid target. She answers with the same select, attack-swing and spell packets she uses on monsters, and the nearest attacker wins, monster or player. She never starts a fight with a player, does not loot one, and drops the fight when that player dies, leaves, vanishes, stops being a valid target or stops attacking her. She walks after a player only to a spot that is not in the other faction's land and no more than 30 yards from where she stood when attacked; otherwise she answers from where she stands. She does not follow a party leader of the other faction. Human characters keep the normal PvP rules. Built and unit-tested, merged without a live playtest; a player who only damages her with spells while selecting someone else is not answered yet.
- **playerbots-quiet-fight** (~100%) — A bot no longer stands beside a monster for good because of a spell she can never press as she is equipped, such as Shoot with no wand or Auto Shot with no bow; she walks in and melees instead, and still presses that spell once she can. Most level-1 mage bots used to stand still in their first fight for the rest of the session over Shoot. A fight that stands five seconds without a swing, a spell, a turn or a walk now writes one line in `Playerbots.log` saying which wait holds her and what the cast check says about each damage spell she knows.
- **playerbots-melee-facing** (~100%) — A bot in melee range turns to face her target with `CMSG_MOVE_SET_FACING` when it is not in front of her, because the server lands a swing only on a target in front. She turns from her feet, not while walking, rooted or stunned. A level-1 mage with no spell to press used to swing at a moth beside or behind her for 40 seconds without killing it, and die.
- **playerbots-talk-while-held** (~100%) — A bot that the server roots or stuns may still accept or hand in a quest from a quest giver already within reach of her feet, as a stunned player can; she does not walk or turn for it, and a fear, confusion or server move still stops everything. Every new Undead is stunned in her grave until she takes Agatha's first quest, and 141 of 145 Undead bots used to stand there at level 1 for good.
- **playerbots-triggered-damage** (~100%) — A bot now counts a spell as a damage spell when its damage comes from a spell it launches, such as Frostbolt, whose only effect fires the missile that hits. Mage bots used to know Frostbolt and never press it, so a level-1 mage fought with Fire Blast every twelve seconds and her staff, and died far more often than any other class.
- **all custom classes require client modifications** —
- **reaper-class** (~55%) — Conquest of Azeroth's Reaper is class 17 in `modules/mod-coa`, with five enabled races, native Runic Power, three specializations plus the required initial specialization (610–613), and class skill 1311. The first-rank combat loop, source visuals and starting equipment are implemented by the reaper-combat-effects job. `.coa reaper make` and `.coa reaper unmake` remain available; playerbots make Reapers only with `Playerbots.CoAClasses = 1`. Full rank progression, talent/specialization completion, replacement of placeholder class stats and broader native UI/gameplay acceptance remain unfinished. See [the module contract](modules/mod-coa/README.md).
- **reaper-combat-effects** (~85%) — The bounded first-rank Reap, Murder, Soul Strike and Soulrend loop uses native Runic Power and owned soul auras, with captured CoA artwork and 3D effects, corrected soul lifetime and retail damage scaling. New characters receive the source Faded Scythe and Phantom outfit through native loadouts. A separate class-preview outfit and selection flourish are prepared. The owner has tested the crow/gesture, limited damage and starting gear; showcase rendering and broader race, resource and visual-cleanup checks remain. See [the module contract](modules/mod-coa/README.md).
- **hero-class** (~40%) — Conquest of Azeroth's Hero as class 16, in its own module `modules/mod-hero` rather than beside Reaper, because Hero is a container for a classless character and the Free Pick economy that will fill it has nothing to do with Reaper. Class id, the stat tables, and its own column choice in the client game tables: Druid's, not Reaper's, because Death Knight's base mana is zero at every level and Hero shows a mana bar. Hotfix rows for the class, ten power types, one hidden specialisation, a class skill line, and every weapon proficiency. Selectable on the creation screen for all thirty-one races a Warrior can be, including Dracthyr, with start positions copied from each race's Warrior. Armour is every type including shields; its specialisations are now the hero-specializations job below. A Hero can learn another class's ability and use it: a borrowed Frostbolt cast, applied Chilled and hit for 52. Its specialisation moved from 9101 to 1479, and later to 600, to fix a spellbook that drew every spell as the wrong one, and every other custom id then followed the same rule: power types to 90 to 99, races to 430 to 460, the class skill line to 1310 and the seventeen skill rows to 1200 to 1216. Nothing was misbehaving because of those four tables, so that move is a precaution against a proved failure mode rather than a fix. Playerbots make Hero bots only with `Playerbots.HeroClass = 1` (off by default; see playerbot-creation-switches). No spells of its own and no starting kit, and Free Pick and Wildcard exist only as empty modes so far (see hero-specializations), which is the point of the class. A Hero now builds Rage from its own weapon swings: that reward was gated on the bar a character displays, which for a Hero is always Mana, so it is now also given to our own classes that hold Rage, leaving every class the client ships untouched. Its other five resources already worked without any change, measured from the values the server saved for four live Heroes: Focus and Energy sit at full, Soul Shards rests at three, and Combo Points comes from the borrowed ability itself. Showing them all on screen is not built. It no longer sits at the client's ceiling of ten power slots: see the hero-extra-powers job below.
- **hero-extra-powers** (~85%) — A Hero holds all twenty-one resources, not the ten the client has room for. The other eleven — Runes, Runic Power, Lunar Power, Holy Power, Maelstrom, Chi, Insanity, Arcane Charges, Fury, Pain and Essence — live on the server beside the ten, which are untouched. The core learned that a resource can live somewhere other than the ten slots the client reads, and after that everything already in the server follows: spells check them, spend them and give them back, auras fire on them, and they regenerate at the rate their own data states. Runes work as runes, six of them on cooldowns, because every rune check now asks whether this character has runes instead of asking whether it is a Death Knight. `mod-hero` owns the rest: which resources those are, what their maximums are, saving and loading them with the character, and telling a panel addon what every one of the twenty-one is worth. Nothing is free and nothing was swapped out: a Hero pays real Runic Power for Death Coil out of Runic Power it built. The client is never told these resources exist, because it has nowhere to put them; with the cost gate patch in `evryLoader` applied to the running game it stops refusing such a cast locally and lets the server decide. Still open: the panel itself, which is Lua and a separate job, and whether any of the eleven should be tuned for a Hero rather than behaving exactly as its own data says.
- **hero-specializations** (~95%) — A Hero picks one of three modes on the normal specialisation page, from level one: Free Pick, Free Pick — Seasonal and Wildcard, plus a hidden Initial that every new Hero starts on. They are real specialisation rows with ids 600 to 603, inside the range the client ships; Initial moved there from 1479. The Activate button is the client's own: it casts the hidden spell 200749, which the client only uses once that spell's skill line row admits the player's class, so a hotfix adds Hero's class bit to the row and `mod-hero` gives every Hero the spell at creation and login. Two changes in `Player.cpp` let a Hero keep a mode below level ten and stop a switch from removing another class's spell that a Hero learned. The server's normal switch saves and loads action bars per mode. The modes grant nothing yet, no spells, talents, currency or acquisition, and a Hero's learned spells are shared by all three. The client half is in `evryLoader`: the specialisation page fixes, a guard in Blizzard's micro menu and `/evryspec`. Checked in game: activating, all six directions between the modes, the spellbook in every mode on a Hero with borrowed spells, per-mode action bars, a cast, a log-out and log-in, the names, and a new Hero getting the activation spell without help. Still to check: the Activate buttons during a cast and in combat, a client and server restart, an ordinary class and Reaper's spec page, and a clean exit. See `modules/mod-hero/README.md`.
